Reading is a physical act

A contrast ratio tells you whether two colors are far enough apart to be separated by the eye. It does not tell you whether the result is comfortable over a page of text.
Read the notes Browse palettes Export (disabled)

A pairing can clear every threshold and still feel harsh, or sit close to the limit and read easily because the hues are related and the counters stay open. Set the size and weight you actually plan to ship, then read this the way a reader would: quickly, at arm's length, without looking for problems.

Watch the line endings and the gaps between words. If your eye keeps returning to the same line, the issue is usually spacing or weight rather than color. If the text seems to vibrate against its background, the two colors are close in lightness but far apart in hue, and no amount of extra size will fix it. Long passages punish decisions that look fine in a headline: thin weights lose their stems, saturated backgrounds bleed into the letterforms, and near-black on near-white can read as cold rather than clear.
