1o POSITIONS ON THE OPEN QUESTIONS

The big Lc figure answers "what is the weakest text on this page, and where". It is the lowest Lc among elements that carry text, named beneath it. The pair's own Lc drops to a one-line footnote: the pair is now an input, not the result. When nothing fails, the figure is still the floor, and the caption says which element sets it.

Palette change. A placed colour is stored as a slot (P2), not a hex. Switching from Analogous to Triad keeps "buttons = P2" and the buttons take the new P2; the ledger row stays. All schemes in the app have five slots, so no slot goes missing. If a future palette has fewer, the element falls back to its default role and its ledger row reads "P5 · not in palette · reset", kept for one session so the visitor sees what happened.

Pair colours as targets. Yes. T and G sit at the end of the chip row and can be dropped like any slot, so a visitor can put the text colour on a button ground or the ground colour on a card. The pair itself is still set only by the two fields; dropping onto the page never changes T or G.

A fourth role. The grown page wants one. Buttons, nav, table numbers and form labels are neither body nor mono; they are UI text at 13–15px, usually heavier, and their Lc thresholds differ from body copy. Recommendation: add UI as a fourth segment in 1b (four segments still fit 300px at 11px labels), defaulting to the body face at 15/600/1.2. Ship three first if the column is tight; the segment control grows without redesign.

Lc values shown are computed with APCA 0.0.98G from the actual colours on the artboards. Thresholds used: 90 preferred body, 75 body minimum, 60 large or bold text, 45 spot text and UI; "–" marks an element that passes only at the large-text threshold.
